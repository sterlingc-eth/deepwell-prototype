The problem

The knowledge exists. It's just in the wrong place.

A mid-sized shop closes 3,000 jobs a year. Each one leaves a trail — but the trail is split across a filing cabinet, a dispatcher's inbox, a shared drive, and a tech's phone.

In the office

The filing cabinet

Signed work orders, manufacturer warranty registrations, permit copies. Complete, and unsearchable. The serial number you need is on page two of a carbon copy.

In the inbox

The PDF nobody renamed

Distributor invoices, startup sheets, equipment submittals — every one attached to an email titled "Re: Re: Fwd: Tuesday." Findable only by the person who received it.

In the truck

The photo on a phone

The clearest record of what was installed is a nameplate photo taken by a tech who has since moved to another company. It was never uploaded anywhere.

How it works

Every record goes two places at once.

One upload, two destinations.

Diagram of the DeepWell data flow. A document is uploaded. The page itself is written to the vault under a key scoped to the account, while the facts about it are written to the index as a single record that holds the key. Later, a question queries the index, the matching record’s key is followed to the vault, and the original page is returned with the answer as the cited source.

  1. 01

    A record arrives

    Any format, from anywhere.

  2. 02

    It goes two places at once

    The page itself is stored under a key scoped to your account. What it says is recorded separately, alongside that key.

  3. 03

    Later, someone asks

    The question is answered from the record, and the key leads back to the original page — attached to the answer as its source.
