The problem

The knowledge exists. It's just in the wrong place.

A mid-sized shop closes 3,000 jobs a year. Each one leaves a trail — but the trail is split across a filing cabinet, a dispatcher's inbox, a shared drive, and a tech's phone.

In the office

The filing cabinet

Signed work orders, manufacturer warranty registrations, permit copies. Complete, and unsearchable. The serial number you need is on page two of a carbon copy.

In the inbox

The PDF nobody renamed

Distributor invoices, startup sheets, equipment submittals — every one attached to an email titled "Re: Re: Fwd: Tuesday." Findable only by the person who received it.

In the truck

The photo on a phone

The clearest record of what was installed is a nameplate photo taken by a tech who has since moved to another company. It was never uploaded anywhere.

A claim denied because nobody registered it

Most manufacturers cut the parts warranty in half if a unit isn't registered within 60–90 days of install — Heil/Tempstar/Comfortmaker and Armstrong Air/AirEase/Ducane drop from 10 yr registered to 5 yr unregistered (based on published manufacturer registration windows). Miss it, and a year-6 compressor or heat-exchanger failure — often $1,500–$4,000 in parts (illustrative) — lands on the bill instead of the manufacturer's.

Your numbers will differ. DeepWell watches every expiry — bell alert plus daily digest — then drafts the customer's extended-warranty email for an admin to approve and send, or run fully automatic. Both ship today.
